### Prepared intake

Launch defaults: ACT/Canberra, ten licensed CRM imports/day, zero paid research budget, minimum source score 50. Geography, industry, volume, provider plan and AI research reservation ceiling are editable with authentication/CSRF protection. Saved geography/industry/score/volume limits apply at licensed candidate import. A score is a deterministic source-completeness heuristic, not proof of customer interest. Provider selection is a planning boundary; it does not activate a service. Existing CSV approval requires licence URL, permitted-use statement, reviewer, attribution and expiry. These are owner attestations, not independently verified contracts. The ABN parser rejects XML entities and never infers an email or marketing consent. Tests use fictional ACT records only.

## Email: SES architecture prepared, live transport unavailable

Amazon SES Sydney is a candidate after use-case approval. [AWS acceptable-use policy](https://aws.amazon.com/aup/) restricts unsolicited mass email; SES production access is not blanket permission for cold outreach. [Sandbox rules](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/request-production-access.html) limit sending to verified destinations or simulator addresses, with regional quotas. The current [SES price page](https://aws.amazon.com/ses/pricing/) lists Essentials first-tier US$0.16/1,000 emails and new-account plan rules; attachments, inbound handling, other AWS services and optional features can add charges. Verify the selected account plan before budgeting.

`email_preparation.py` provides:

- Injected provider/DNS checks for verified domain, DKIM CNAME/status, custom MAIL FROM, SPF and enforced DMARC. It cannot certify authentication from a typed address. A real bounded DNS/SES identity collector, DNS propagation checks, strict DMARC alignment and MAIL FROM MX validation remain to be connected.
- Consent-evidence expiry and suppression checks; the conservative pilot policy accepts explicit consent only. Existing correspondence history, follow-up stopping, duplicate/rate gates and mock bounce/complaint/reply handling are retained.
- Plain-text MIME with identity/contact footer and HTTPS one-click unsubscribe headers. Hashed, expiring opt-out tokens; POST immediately suppresses without login, including during Emergency Pause. GET only offers confirmation. Tokens last 90 days, beyond the 30-day minimum operational requirement.
- Injected SES v2 SDK request contract limited to five AWS simulator destination names in Sydney, disabled by default. Tests use a fake SDK client. **No AWS simulator delivery was attempted.** No production email send executor exists.
- SNS v2 Notification canonical signature/topic/time/certificate-origin checks with injected certificate loading, tested with generated certificates. The caller must use HTTPS CA/hostname validation, bounded reads and no redirects to the fixed trusted AWS host. A real certificate fetcher, subscription handshake, durable SNS dedup/event-to-message mapping, SES bounce/complaint ingestion, inbound receipt storage and sender-check refresh are **not connected**. Existing mock signed events are not SES verification.

There is no general SES SendEmail idempotency token. A future dispatcher must reserve an exact approved message before submission, mark uncertain outcomes for manual/provider reconciliation and avoid automatic resend. It must recheck sender evidence, current consent/suppression, recent correspondence, quota and pause immediately before the request. Persist provider message ID separately from delivered status; acceptance is not delivery.

Legal review remains necessary. [ACMA guidance](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) requires consent, correct sender identity/contact details and functional unsubscribe, with requests actioned within five working days. A public email address alone is not automatic consent. [OAIC direct marketing guidance](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/direct-marketing) also explains how APP 7 interacts with the Spam Act; other privacy obligations may still apply. This review is not legal certification. Authentication design follows [SES DMARC guidance](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/send-email-authentication-dmarc.html) and [SNS signature guidance](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sns/latest/dg/sns-verify-signature-of-message.html).

## AI: shared approval and conservative reservations

Local saved-record research, drafting templates, reply rules, quote arithmetic, fixed website generation/revisions and deterministic QA work without an AI account. Optional AI can help synthesize evidence and draft outreach, replies, proposals and site text. New `research-assist` and `qa-assist` jobs return review-only suggestions; they cannot approve research, change deterministic QA, publish or contact anyone.

Every paid route requires explicit model/provider configuration, current owner-reviewed AUD input/output rates, a positive daily budget/request ceiling and an exact expiring approval tied to inputs, model, rates and provider/key fingerprint. Input bytes plus framing and output allowances are reserved conservatively before transport. Research assistance additionally requires a positive Launch research ceiling. Unknown outcomes retain reservations; no automatic paid retry. Production requires an explicit provider hostname allowlist and supports key files. Legacy AI entry points cannot mint the one-use transport capability. This is reservation accounting, not provider-bill reconciliation.

[GPT-5.4 Mini documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5.4-mini) currently lists US$0.75 input / US$4.50 output per million tokens, Chat Completions support and a 10% regional processing uplift. Example only: 10,000 input plus 1,000 output tokens = US$0.012 before taxes, FX and any uplift. No model was selected/enabled. Model-specific request compatibility (including token/reasoning fields), quality, data residency/retention, account access and actual billed-token bounds require an approved account sandbox evaluation before paid use. Do not copy USD rates into AUD-cent fields. Existing tests use a synthetic model and mocked HTTP only.

## Website delivery

Local staging and a private handover ZIP are implemented. Approved static versions are rechecked for deterministic QA, active markup, file hashes and symlinks; export links are rewritten to static filenames. Rollback rechecks files at execution. Local daily maintenance records integrity alerts.

The Launch preparation route produces an AWS Sydney static-hosting request plan for an exact approved version and an owner-attested customer permission reference. It includes a manifest and explicit `public_execution_authorized=false`. S3 upload execution, private versioned bucket resources, CloudFront, domain/DNS mutation, certificate issuance, contact-form endpoint, public-origin monitoring and customer access management are **not implemented/connected**. Separate credentials, customer permission, resource budget and launch approval are required. Preparing a JSON plan is not a tested hosting integration. No public website was created.

## Payments and bank feeds

Stripe Checkout is restricted to test keys and synthetic invoices; signatures, paid status, test-mode evidence, changed-content replay and canonical payment-intent dedup are regression tested with injected responses. Existing partial mock payments, owner-recorded reconciliation, synthetic refunds/disputes and encrypted bank settings remain available. Blank bank inputs preserve encrypted values. A signed event is not a bank settlement report.

[Stripe Australian pricing](https://stripe.com/au/pricing), fetched directly on the review date, shows 1.65% + A$0.30 for domestic online cards and 3.5% + A$0.30 international, with conversion/additional product fees possible. Cached search snippets still showed older domestic pricing; use the live account quote before activation. No Stripe request or charge was made. Actual account test events, asynchronous payment states, charge/refund/dispute correlation, settlement/balance reconciliation and refunds across multiple provider object types need provider sandbox certification before live collection.

Automated bank feeds are not implemented. A bank/provider contract, account-holder consent, bank-feed access rights, retention review and verified transaction identifiers would be necessary. Manual evidence remains explicitly owner-recorded and separate from provider verification. Real invoice issuance/delivery, subscriptions and verified MRR are unavailable. Confirm business identity, ABN, GST and actual bank details privately before a future approved live implementation; no real details were invented.
